experiments in pioneer churches, as well as anecdotal evidence, suggest that it is vital to help people integrate their everyday activities beyond the church into the way they engage with God—whether Bible reading, or prayer, or re-flection, or habits and practices that serve to create a conscious engagement of daily life with Christ.

It could be discussing work issues with a prayer partner, it could be belonging to a small group where frontline and work concerns are part of the culture, it could be always praying for their work when they drive past the oak tree by the builder’s merchants on the A71, or asking the Lord ‘to establish the work of their hands’ every time they wash them (Ps 90.17).

So one of the ways to nurture workplace disciples is to encourage them to integrate workplace concerns into their own prayer lives. And one of the ways to begin that is to integrate workplace concerns into the church’s corporate praying.
